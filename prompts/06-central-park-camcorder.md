## The dress

Read from the product photo and written as construction rather than vibe, because "black
floral mini dress" returns a different garment every seed. The load-bearing details are the
**ditsy scale** of the print (small scattered daisies, not large blooms), the **ruched sheer
band** across the top of the bodice with its centre tie, the **waist seam at the natural
waist**, and the **full circle skirt** — that last one is what makes it flare and swing rather
than hang straight.

Now recorded in CHARACTER-BIBLE §2 as her second locked outfit, so it can be reused verbatim.

## The camcorder

Two decisions worth keeping:

**`plain and unbranded`** — positive phrasing. Asking for no logos would name logos, and these
models put a mangled trademark on anything they are told not to brand.

**`the flip-out screen swung open and angled away from the camera so only its back panel
shows`** — screens are where this fails. A visible LCD gets a fabricated interface, usually a
melted one, and it sits right in the middle of the frame. Angling it away keeps the flip-screen
silhouette, which is the whole visual point of a camcorder, while removing the thing that
breaks.

## The pose: still, not staged

An earlier version had her mid-stride, half turned, elbows flared and the skirt swinging. It
read as performed. Motion is the wrong tool here — a person operating a camcorder is
**concentrating, and therefore still.**

What replaced it:

| Was | Now |
|---|---|
| Mid-stride, half turned away | Standing still and upright, square to the camera |
| Elbows out, shoulders lifted | Elbows tucked in against her ribs |
| One heel off the ground | Weight even on both feet |
| Skirt swinging from the turn | Skirt hanging still |
| Motion blur on hands and hem | Removed — a still subject has none |

The candid quality now comes from attention rather than action: **her eyes are on the screen,
not on the lens.** That alone separates a person doing something from a person being
photographed, and it survives at any energy level.

**On `close to her face`** — the camcorder sits just below her chin with the open screen tilted
up toward her, so her whole face stays clear above it. Held any higher it crops the face this
project has spent every prompt locking down, and identity is worth more than the extra inch.

## Dappled light

The elm tunnel over the Mall is the reason to shoot there, and it is also a real trap. Dappled
light is specified as **hard-edged patches that blow out where they land**, with the rest in
open shade — not a soft golden wash. Soft dapple is a render default; blown patches with sharp
edges are what a phone sensor actually does under a canopy, and the contrast is what sells it.

## What to check

1. **Identity** — brows, the mole above her left eyebrow, grey iris with its darker ring.
2. **The dress construction** — ditsy print scale, ruched band with the centre tie, waist seam,
   circle skirt actually flaring.
3. **Body proportions** — narrow waist and shoulders against full hips and bust. The block most
   often silently averaged away.
4. **Hands and the camcorder.** Both hands on a small object is a hard case: count fingers,
   check the wrist strap goes somewhere, check the screen shows its back panel.
5. **Feet and sandals** — full-length framing means both are visible. Toes are a common failure.
6. **Background faces stay indistinct.**
7. **Skin** — pores present, sheen broken into small glints, tone even, face hairless, no lines
   beyond what the pose folds.
8. **Age** reads mid-twenties (PROJECT-BRIEF §7).
